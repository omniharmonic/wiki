# Awakening the Earth Dharma (Part I)

*This is the first part of a [[awakening-the-earth-dharma-part-ii|two part]] series. This series represents ideas that are not my own, synthesized from my own positionality, in service of what’s emerging. I owe all of the ideas in this essay to my friend and mentor [Samantha Sweetwater](https://www.samanthasweetwater.com/) whose book, [True Human](https://www.amazon.com/True-Human-Reimagining-Ourselves-World/dp/B0G2HSJNWG), articulates most of them far more thoroughly than I do here. I strongly encourage my readers to purchase her book and participate in her courses to go deeper on this subject. I am also indebted to my dear friend, [Charles Eisenstein](https://charleseisenstein.org/), for laying the foundations that make this essay possible. I also extend my profound gratitude to elders and wayshowers across history for their contributions to the awakening of the earth dharma: Joanna Macy, Thich Nhat Hahn, Martin Luther King Jr, Vandana Shiva, Chief Oren Lyons, John Churchill, John Trudell, Teilhard De Chardin, Barbara Marx Hubbard, Winona LaDuke, Bill Plotkin, Sri Aurobindo, The Mother, Wendell Berry, Casey Camp, Daniel Schmachtenberger, John Vervaeke, and others.*

---

We are living inside the compression of collapse and the opening of what comes after. The old world has not yet finished dying, the new one has not yet been born, and in that gap, the dominant system is doing what dying systems must do: consolidate. Tighten. Build the surveillance apparatus. Race toward the exponential technologies it believes will finally solve the problem of its own incoherence. We are watching the authoritarian reflex of a civilization that feels its contradictions closing in.

These contradictions are structural. They’re what happens over a long enough period of time when a cosmology is fundamentally misaligned with the processes that sustain and nourish the continuity of life. Despite how it may seem, the cosmology of our current civilization is coming apart at the seams. The contradictions are becoming too loud to ignore. People are looking for another story, even if they don’t yet know what it looks like. The story they are looking for is not new. It is the oldest story there is. It has been carried, in some form, by every indigenous tradition, by every contemplative lineage, by every grandmother who knew that the world was alive and watching. It was buried. Driven underground by the same trauma patterns that build empires.

Until very recently, it felt like the emerging story of regenerative post-capitalism was a fringe topic discussed only at the margins. Now, [solarpunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solarpunk) [imagination activism](https://www.moralimaginations.com/imaginationactivism) is beginning to go mainstream. Many of us are noticing and describing the new story that’s emerging. Can you feel it? Is it showing up in subtle ways in your Instagram [feed](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DVWOFWHjuPR/?igsh=MXBwdXNnbW5yOW1taA==) too or is that just my own biases encoded into the attention-extracting algorithm? It’s hard to tell because the regeneration will not be televised. It requires a deeper questioning of the incentives and purpose of our current economy and institutions than is allowed in polite corporate company, but I believe its whispers are beginning to coalesce around dining room tables and in GenZ group chats.

This essay is about that new story and what it requires from us, who we must become to meet this moment and live the new story together, to sing our part in the larger song. I believe it’s important to give it a name while simultaneously seeing it as something that transcends any sense of direct individual authorship. I believe this is the Earth waking up to itself and it’s something all of us get to participate in and shape as our birthright, as organisms who incarnated on this planet as humanity passed between fundamental stories about the nature of reality.

The planetary crisis we face becomes the doorway, but to truly step through it, we need to strip ourselves of the conditioned belief systems that have internalized even the crisis itself. The story that most of us have been handed about the climate crisis is, in a quiet but decisive way, wrong. It’s not really a climate crisis. It’s an ecological crisis, and beneath that, a crisis of relationship. The atmosphere, the soil, the water, and our own psyches are the final ledgers, the places where the debt comes due. But the debt itself was accumulated through the slow, centuries-long degradation of the web of life: the microbiome, the forests, the watersheds, the kinship bonds, the languages that knew the names of local rivers, the ceremonies that kept the cycles intact. The carbon statistics are a symptom. The disease is a severed relationship with reality itself.

This is what makes our moment a *planetary initiation* rather than merely a policy problem. An initiation is what happens when the continuation of who you have been starts to threaten the continuation of the larger life you are embedded within. You cannot negotiate your way through it. You cannot optimize your way through it. You can only pass through it by becoming someone, or, at this scale, some civilization, capable of a different relationship to the whole.

Right now, it looks as though the old story is winning. It’s forming a surveillance apparatus that threatens to lock us all into authoritarian preservation of the current order as the contradictions intensify. Desperate, dying empires build greater and greater fortresses. They race toward technological solutions to problems that are fundamentally ontological. And the people driving these technologies are, by and large, some of the least wise people on the planet, not because they lack intelligence, but because they are among the most severed from the relational understanding that would allow them to wield such power responsibly. The closest historical parallel is nuclear weapons, but those were containable: only large nation-states could build them, with enormous investments of time and capital. Mutually assured destruction kept our suicidal tendencies at bay. Artificial intelligence isn’t like that. It’s uncontainable. It will leak out of the lab the way the [COVID-19](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cd9qjjj4zy5o) virus did, created from the same spirit of blind curiosity, the same impulse to explore a frontier without any corresponding understanding of how it relates to everything else. While some work tirelessly to either ensure AI alignment, constrain the multi-polar trap racing to misaligned AGI, or [ring the alarm bell](https://www.focusfeatures.com/the-ai-doc-or-how-i-became-an-apocaloptimist) about AI acceleration, I fear that all of these strategies are insufficient to actually meet what’s coming. The game theory driving the proliferation of misaligned AI is unstoppable. As such, the only remaining strategy is for humanity to become an aligned species. This reality is both terrifying and liberating. How could our entire species spontaneously awaken from the slumber of separation? It seems almost impossible to even imagine.

And yet. Something in us knows. I believe there is something innate inside of human beings that will resist this foreclosure of the living world and the consolidation of authoritarian power. I believe these are the final moves of late stage extraction that precede mass awakening rather than dystopian singularity. More and more people are waking up to the existential failure of our current story every day, even if they cannot yet name what they are waking up to. It matters that those of us who care about a more beautiful world make explicit the specifics of the story that we’re standing for, to give it a name. Because the more that we align through this new and ancient story, the more that we create a coherent experience of reality. A coherent field. And the more coherent that field becomes, the more possible it is for others to find their way in.

This essay is an attempt to name that story plainly. **I did not invent any of it.** This builds upon the work of Joanna Macy and Thich Nhat Hanh and Vandana Shiva and Charles Eisenstein and Samantha Sweetwater and many, many unnamed and unknown others who have carried this knowing through the long dark of industrial civilization. I am one of countless people trying to lift it into the particular idiom our moment requires. What makes this story different from a traditional religious or metaphysical worldview is that it has to do with our immanent embeddedness. It doesn’t require any explicitly metaphysical claims in order to be validated. It is available to anyone through the direct phenomenological experience that we have as incarnate beings within living systems. You do not need to believe anything. You only need to *pay attention*.

## The Dharma of Embeddedness

Earth dharma is just one way of saying something that others have more accurately and completely described before me. Samantha Sweetwater calls it [Gaia Dharma](https://www.samanthasweetwater.com/post/design-a-stunning-blog). John Churchill calls it [Planetary Dharma](https://planetarydharma.substack.com/p/co-creating-the-third-attractor). Sri Aurobindo called it a gnostic society. Teilhard de Chardin called it the “noosphere.” It draws from Engaged Buddhism, complexity science, Liberation theology, and indigenous wisdom traditions. It lives in the unnamable center of our [[wiki/concepts/interbeing|interbeing]], our mutual embeddedness and the mutual responsibility that flows through it.

Naming in and of itself is problematic, collapsing something so sacred and fundamentally indescribable into words. I don’t intend to coin “earth dharma” as yet another branded teaching or series of proprietary workshops. I chose that name so as not to encroach upon the thought leadership of others whom I deeply respect. But ultimately, it’s what the words *point toward* that matters. I don’t care what term we use, just that we collectively orient towards shared naming for the story emerging in the space between us. I am an amplifier of this story, not its author. And, I believe earth dharma is a sufficient description of what I see humanity evolving into.

The term *dharma* is drawn from the vedic cosmovision, understood by both Hindu and Buddhist traditions. Dharma is often contrasted with *karma*. Karma, the law of duality, the law of cause and effect, is merely a reflection of the reality that our actions create ripples, that we are bound by the consequences of what we do and what was done before us. Karma is the weight of accumulated misalignment. It is the trauma body of our species, the residue of generations of acting without awareness of how our actions land in the fabric of things. We are always, whether we know it or not, accountable to it.

Dharma is what becomes available when awareness catches up with that accountability. As we become more aware of the impact of our actions, we can begin to take right action. But dharma is not right action in a personal, abstract, or even moralistic sense, the kind of rightness you arrive at through rules and principles or categorical imperatives. It is right action as an embedded participant in the wholeness of reality. It is what happens when you actually perceive, not just understand intellectually, the harmonic, rhythmic, cyclical interbeing of things. When the layers of conditioning thin out enough that you can see the natural pattern of how things fit together, how they co-arise, how the water feeds the soil feeds the root feeds the tree feeds the air feeds the rain. And through that awareness, the only choice, the choiceless choice, is to tend to it. To steward it. To participate in its continuity.

*The Tao*, *ma’at*, these are names for the natural harmonic order of Earth and the cosmos, an order we already exist within. Dharma is what happens when a being becomes aware enough of that order to move with it rather than against it. It’s not a rule you follow. It is the *Wu Wei*, the natural movement of a being that has remembered what it is part of.

And the planetary crisis we are in is, in the most broad sense, a karmic reckoning. We are meeting the accumulated consequences of centuries of acting without awareness of how our actions ripple into the water, the soil, the bodies of other beings, the bodies of our children. The dharmic response is not guilt. Guilt is still a self-referential loop, the separated self flagellating itself, still orbiting around its own experience. The dharmic response is to widen awareness until the way forward becomes obvious, until right action is no longer something you have to figure out, but something you can feel. Until tending and stewarding the world becomes simply what it feels like to be alive.

This is what I mean by earth dharma. Awakening to it is both the most personal work there is and the most collective work there is. Because the personal work, the healing, the undoing of conditioning, the restoration of our capacity to actually perceive reality, is what makes the collective work possible. And the collective work, the building of institutions and cultures and economies that reflect our actual embeddedness, is what creates the conditions for more people to do the personal work. These are not separate projects. They are one recursive loop. Understanding that loop is foundational to how we participate in it.

## The Story of Separation Lives in the Body

To understand why Western civilization is so catastrophically misaligned with living systems, we have to go deeper than economics or ideology or even culture. We have to go into the body. Into the nervous system. Into the intergenerational patterning that shapes how we perceive and respond to the world before we have any conscious understanding of it.

The [[wiki/concepts/story-of-separation|story of separation]] is not just a bad idea that took hold. It is a trauma response, operating at civilizational scale.

What is incumbent upon us as people rising to this planetary initiation is to interrogate how the worldview of separation, which is to say of colonization and imperialism, lives within us. Not just out there, in the structures. In us. In our bodies, our reflexes, our patterns of relating. Because the history of colonization is the history of trauma, and that history did not end when the ships stopped sailing. It continues, in and through the body, between bodies, across generations.

At some point in our distant collective ancestry, there was a traumatizing experience that caused a fundamental rupture in right relationship, a rupture that would cause a part of our species to become cannibalistic, to continuously seek to consume, to fill an emptiness that cannot actually be filled through material safety. It’s the force deeper than game theory that drives the evolution of empires: fear. They do not feel safe in the world. That they would need to develop ever-more-expansive armaments in order to protect themselves from a threat that their own behavior creates tells us something specific about the nature of the wound. Every armament is a confession of insecurity. Every expansion of territorial control is a flight from a felt sense of not-belonging that no amount of weapons or wealth or technological mastery can heal.

This is the pattern that Eastern and Indigenous traditions across the world named long before Western psychology caught up. Buddhism speaks of the hungry ghost, with its enormous stomach and pinhole mouth, forever consuming, never satisfied. The Algonquian and Cree peoples call it *wetiko*, the cannibalistic spirit that colonizes consciousness itself. The pattern is self-concealing: the cannibal spirit hides itself from recognition, colonizing not just behavior but the very framework through which behavior is evaluated. You cannot see it because it has become the lens through which you see.

What makes this recognition practical rather than merely diagnostic is the observation that the same pattern lives inside each of us. Intergenerational trauma is not a metaphor. It is a measurable, felt inheritance that travels through bloodlines, through epigenetics, through the quality of the attachment bond between parent and child. At many points in our genetic history and in our own lives, we have traumatic experiences which create fundamental psychological ruptures. Particularly in early childhood, we all encounter moments where we did not receive the attunement, the love, the safety we needed. It did not have to be dramatic. It only had to be enough. And in response, we developed maladaptive or misaligned hungry ghost strategies, mostly unconscious, that are attempting to keep us safe from experiencing that trauma again by closing off connection and consuming what we fear we don’t have enough of.

This is where the story of separation actually occurs phenomenologically, in the body. It lives in the feeling of not belonging. Of not feeling safe in the world. Of needing to develop evermore sophisticated strategies in order to get love, or feel belonging, or feel like we have enough. Those strategies close us off. They fundamentally close the heart, because we are trying to protect some part of ourselves. And as long as we are trying to protect ourselves, we can’t actually be in connection with reality as it is. We are in contact with our defenses. We are living inside a story about reality that was constructed by a wounded child to survive, and we are mistaking it for reality itself.

Now scale that up. The aggregate of billions of people running their defenses, optimizing their lives around wounds they cannot name, becomes an economy of extraction. An arms race in AI and military technology. An “economic development” paradigm driven by the same *wetiko* that drives ecocide. Resource extraction itself is this kind of emergent synthesis: our collective, unprocessed grief and unprocessed lack of safety, mobilized as economic engine. The endless consumption, the races to exponential technologies, the desperate need to control and predict and secure, all of this is the civilizational expression of the same wound that lives in your chest when you worry about not having enough money in your account to protect and provide for the people you love. The same protective closure. The same inability to fully rest in the world as it actually is.

And so the most intimate ground of our own nervous system, the site where we carry our own piece of this larger trauma body, is also the site where the regenerative civilization either becomes possible or does not. This inner work must be distinguished from its false twin of narcissistic self-improvement. It is not a luxury for the privileged. It is the infrastructure of belonging. It is the ground on which everything else either stands or falls.

## Initiation, Death, and the Relational Self

If we keep pulling the thread on the collective trauma body, we arrive at the deepest interior of the fear that drives the crises we currently inhabit. At its core, infinite growth on a finite planet is an expression of the same escapist fantasy of the transhumanist project, the denial of death and the aim to live forever, to escape Earth, to perpetually self-optimize.

A fragmented self, a self that experiences itself as separate, experiences the end of itself as the end of the world. When you are a fragment identifying with the whole, the idea of the end of *me* is an absolute catastrophe, because there is nothing beyond the fragment. There is no continuity to rest in, no larger process to dissolve into. And so the fragment clings. It prolongs. It invests endlessly in the extension of its own duration. Silicon Valley’s obsession with life extension, mind uploading, and cryonics, these are not the overcoming of mortality. They are mortality terror, industrialized. The same hungry ghost, wearing a lab coat. The same desperate attempt to fill an emptiness that cannot be filled, now directed at overcoming the emptiness of death itself.

More broadly, we live in a neotenous culture, a culture that fetishizes youth and neglects initiation. Colonial cultures have no meaningful rites of passage into adulthood. We have no culturally sanctioned process for becoming an elder, no recognized threshold where the concerns of the self give way to the concerns of the whole. We are obsessed with prolonging life and avoiding death. A culture without initiation produces perpetual adolescents wielding civilizational-scale power.

But once we do the deep inner work, the mother-father work, the generational and transpersonal trauma work, the slow composting of the armor that was supposed to keep us safe, we become something different. We become initiated adults. Mature adults. People who can hold complexity, hold paradox, hold grief and beauty and conflict simultaneously without collapsing. And part of that maturation is a transformed relationship with death.

A relational self, a self that has done the work to feel its embeddedness, realizes that we are inside of a process that is also us. We are the river and we are the forest. We grow and we blossom, and then we let it go. And we dissolve back into the totality from which we came. We see this as beautiful not as something we should be afraid of or fight. Death is a return, another turn in an endless cycle.

This is, in one reading, what Christ realized. The recognition that we are the wholeness that we are embedded within. “I and the Father are one” is not a theological claim. It is a phenomenological description of a quality of consciousness available to any being willing to pass through the threshold, the passage through which the individual self dies and the relational self emerges. If we can surrender to that quality of consciousness and acknowledge that we are the totality, that we are the wholeness experiencing itself through this particular aperture, then the fragment that thought it was separate can relax its grip. The crucifixion is the willing surrender of the protective self. The resurrection is what reality looks like when it is no longer obscured by the fragment that thought it was the whole.

The return of Christ consciousness is not an individual event. The conventional eschatological fantasy, a single man descending from the clouds to judge and redeem, is the story of separation projected onto salvation itself, one more hero narrative in which a single sovereign authority rescues the passive many from above. That is not what is happening. What is happening is an emergence. The second coming is not an event but a process. It is the awakening of Christ consciousness *in* human beings, in communities, in relationships, in the living tissue between people who are learning to organize around mutual care rather than domination. Each person who genuinely realizes their interbeing with all existence participates in this emergence. Each community that embodies mutual aid and collective stewardship becomes, in the deepest sense, the body of Christ. A community of people practicing wholeness *is* wholeness, incarnate, in matter, on earth.

The same recognition appears across many traditions, encoded in different language but pointing at the same threshold. Martin Luther King Jr. called it the [[wiki/concepts/beloved-community|beloved community]], the reality that emerges through the realization that “we are caught in an inescapable network of mutuality, tied in a single garment of destiny. Whatever affects one directly, affects all indirectly.” Thich Nhat Hanh, one of the great carriers of the dharma in our time, was asked near the end of his life about the coming of the next Buddha, the Maitreya, the awakened one whose arrival the Buddhist traditions have long anticipated. His answer: the next Buddha will not be an individual. The next Buddha will be the *sangha*: the community.

We have been conditioned to wait for the singular savior, the genius founder, the enlightened master who will show us the way. Every Hollywood arc, every political campaign, every startup mythology reinforces this: change comes from the exceptional individual. The rest of us follow, consume, vote, invest, believe. But Thich Nhat Hanh is saying that awakening is a collective phenomenon. It is not something that happens in isolated individuals who then radiate it outward. It is something that happens *between* beings, in the relational field, in the quality of attention and care and accountability that a community of practice holds together. The sangha, the community of practitioners, is not the *vehicle* for the Buddha’s teaching. The sangha *is* the Buddha. The community *is* the awakening.

This is what I mean when I say that the inner work and the collective work are one recursive loop. You cannot fully awaken alone, because awakening is the realization of embeddedness, and embeddedness is by definition relational. You need others. You need community. You need the friction and the mirror and the holding that only other beings can provide. And the community cannot awaken without individuals doing their own inner work, because a community of armored, defended, trauma-driven people, no matter how beautifully their governance is designed, will reproduce the patterns of domination at the relational level.

Joanna Macy, one of the great guides home for those of us from colonizer lineages, who showed us that ecological crisis is not something to be solved but something to be *grieved through* as a doorway to remembering, tells a story that weaves these threads into a single image. It is her retelling of the [[wiki/concepts/shambhala-warriors|Shambhala Warrior]] prophecy, a Tibetan Buddhist teaching about a time that sounds remarkably like our own.

The prophecy says: there comes a time when all life on earth is in danger. Great barbarian powers have arisen, powers of unprecedented destructive force. And in this time, the kingdom of Shambhala begins to emerge. But Shambhala is not a place. You cannot find it on a map. You cannot march an army there. It exists in the hearts and minds of the Shambhala warriors, and that is where the only defense lies.

These warriors, the prophecy says, must go into the very corridors of power where the barbarian weapons are made. They must go into the citadels of the old story, into the boardrooms and the server farms and the legislatures and the laboratories where the machinery of destruction is being assembled. And they must dismantle those weapons. But they do not do this through violence, because violence is the logic of the old story, and you cannot dismantle the master’s house with the master’s tools without becoming the master yourself. If you fight the cannibal spirit by trying to consume it, you become what you oppose.

Instead, the Shambhala warriors carry two instruments. The first is *compassion*, an inexhaustible compassion born from the grief of seeing what is being destroyed, from the willingness to feel the full weight of the suffering rather than armor against it. It is the kind of compassion that comes from having done the inner work, from having met your own pain and the pain of your lineage and the pain of the world without flinching, without dissociating, without rushing to fix. It is not sentimental. It is fierce. It is the compassion of someone who has let their heart break open and found that the breaking did not destroy them but made them large enough to hold what needs to be held.

And the second instrument is *insight into the radical interconnectedness of all things*. The recognition, not as theory but as felt, embodied knowing that all of these destructive weapons, all of these systems of extraction and control, are *mind-made*. They were created by human minds, and they can be unmade by human minds. They are not features of reality itself. They are constructions that a traumatized species built while dreaming it was separate, and they can be composted and transformed once we wake up from that dream. This insight, into the interdependence of all phenomena, into the fact that nothing exists in isolation, that every weapon and every wound is woven from the same web as every act of care and every moment of beauty, is what gives the warrior the capacity to act without hatred. Because when you see the web clearly enough, you recognize that the people building the machinery of destruction are not enemies. They are possessed, as we have all been possessed to varying degrees, by a consciousness that cannot see what it is destroying. They are trying to solve real problems, death, suffering, insecurity, from within a paradigm that guarantees failure.

These two instruments, compassion and insight into radical interconnectedness, are the same two instruments the earth dharma asks us to cultivate. They are the two faces of the same realization. Compassion is what embeddedness *feels like*. Insight into interconnectedness is what embeddedness *looks like* in practice. Together, they are what it means to be awake. Together, they are what the next Buddha, the sangha, the community of the awakened, is made of.

Initiation walks a person to the threshold where the fragmented self can die, so that the relational self can take its place as an adult in the more-than-human community, to find its place in the sangha.

## The Doorway and the Walking Through

Where death is the ultimate initiation, mystical and indigenous traditions around the world offer initiatory experiences intended to help us die before we die, to lay down the armors of our defenses that we might truly live. Plant medicines are an ally in this process. They can show you, with overwhelming clarity, that the boundary you have taken for the edge of yourself is actually a membrane, porous and participatory, and that you are embedded in a diaphanous totality that is the oneness of everything. They can create an ontological shift so complete that for a few hours, the entire edifice of separation becomes transparently absurd.

Meditation can do it, slowly, over years of patient attention. Grief can do it, the kind of grief that strips you so bare that there is nothing left to defend. Crisis can do it, and often does, because we are often faced with crises precisely to catalyze these ontological shifts. When the old way of seeing stops working, when it starts generating more pain than it prevents, something in the system begins to let go.

In some ways, that is exactly what this planetary initiation is. Life is asking all of us, at once, to let go of a way of seeing that is destroying us. The planetary crisis is the macro-scale version of the personal crisis, the moment when the continuation of the old pattern becomes more dangerous than the terrifying unknown of what lies beyond it.

But plant medicine can only show you the doorway. It can light it up so brilliantly that you cannot deny it exists. But you still have to walk through it. And walking through it requires a different way of showing up. You can understand it cognitively, but unless your actions are a reflection of that embeddedness, unless they come with a kind of compassion, a capacity to hold multiple perspectives, a willingness to get inside someone else’s world, to empathically extend your awareness into the more-than-human, you are still standing in front of the door.

The same is true of the longing itself. You can chase the next ceremony, the next retreat, the next teacher or technique or substance that will finally fix you, finally make you whole. But that longing, directed outward, is still the same extractive pattern wearing spiritual clothes. You cannot consume your way to wholeness. The doorway is showing you where the passage is. But the passage is your life: every interaction, every choice, every conversation with your partner or child.

Planetary consciousness is not a credential you earn through peak experiences. It is something that happens, naturally and inevitably, when you come to deeply understand your mutual embeddedness and begin to live from it. It requires a different quality of attention. It requires doing the thing that the protective self most fears: letting the armor down long enough to actually feel, to stay in connection long enough to build lasting coherence and safety.

## Reparenting Through Earth and Sun

Most of the trauma patterning we carry, we inherited through our parents. And they inherited it through their parents. And they through theirs, stretching back through generations in an unbroken chain of people trying to pass on love through a transmission that had been partially broken long before any of them arrived. The crux of that healing process is encountering, with real honesty, where we did not receive the love that we needed as children from our mother or father. Not to assign blame but to see the pattern clearly enough to have compassion for it. Because what we find, when we look honestly, is that our parents were not the source of our trauma. They were people running their own trauma patterns, inheriting the unprocessed pain of their own parents, doing their best within the constraints of what had been done to them. Being able to hold that compassion, for them, and therefore for the parts of ourselves that are just like them, is part of how the chain begins to break. We can, as adults, bring compassion to our parents for the ways in which they themselves were just inheriting trauma. And that compassion, extended through us to them, heals it within ourselves.

But here we arrive at a deeper challenge. To heal an attachment wound, you generally need to feel what secure attachment feels like. You need a psychospiritual imprint of a healthy union between the mother and the father at a phenomenological level. If you did not receive it, if the transmission was broken before you arrived, it can be very hard to source it. You cannot reason your way into a feeling you have never had. You don’t have the template.

One of the most powerful things I have discovered is that we are already embedded in an immanent reality that reflects a healthy expression of the mother and father relationship. It’s not a metaphor. It’s already here.

The earth is our mother. This is a literal description of our situation. Her body is the substrate of our bodies. Her water is our water. Her food is our food. She provides for us unconditionally. We unconditionally belong to her. Whether or not we acknowledge it, she is holding us up right now, in this very moment, as you read these words. The ground beneath you is her body. We can feel that unconditional love of the mother, the way she provides for us, cares for us, holds us without condition, by actually connecting into the earth itself. By lying on the ground. By putting our hands in the soil. By going barefoot. By feeling, somatically, the fact that there has always, always been ground beneath us.

The sun is our father. It gives of itself unconditionally, pouring out light and heat freely, without attachment, without expectation, without accounting. It does not ask anything in return. It simply gives. It sources the energy that makes life possible, offering that creative force to the mother as an essential ingredient to create life. We can feel that quality of unconditional giving, the father’s presence, steady and radiant and undemanding, by turning our face to the sun and literally receiving it.

The union between the star and the planet is itself a union between the masculine and the feminine archetypal forces. Their holy marriage is what creates the conditions for life to emerge. And we are literally their children. Not figuratively. Not symbolically. Literally. Every atom in your body was forged in a star. Every molecule of water in your blood has cycled through the body of the earth for billions of years. The star and the planet are the transpersonal patterns that the archetypes of mother and father point toward, and they are simultaneously those patterns themselves. They are what I call *literal metaphors*: they point to something, and they *are* the thing they point to. There is no gap between the symbol and the reality.

What is beautiful about this cosmology is that it requires no act of faith. There is nothing to believe. No metaphysical claims are being made. It is phenomenology, not religion. The earth *is* holding you. The sun *is* giving its light. These are facts you can verify right now, with your body, without needing permission from any tradition or authority. And yet this recognition gives us an actual relational field within which to reparent ourselves, to feel, in the body, the unconditional belonging and love we may not have received from our human parents. To feel, in the body, the unconditional gift of creation, the healthy balance between the father and mother that can guide us deeper into our own wholeness.

If we need to resolve these generational traumas, but we did not ourselves receive the love we needed, the earth and the sun can give us the imprint. They can teach us what it feels like. Not as a concept, but as a felt sense in the nervous system, the relaxation, the softening, the sense of being held that is the ground of secure attachment.

When this reparenting begins to take root, something remarkable happens. The protection around the heart starts to relax. Not because you have forced it open at yet another ceremony or personal development retreat, but because it is no longer needed. Something deeper is holding you. Something was always holding you. And when the abandonment wound that drives the protection of the heart begins to heal, the story of separation begins to lose its gravity, the endless drive to consume and to control in order to feel safe, to feel belonging, to feel like we have enough, begins to fade. The engine of separation loses its fuel.

This is the ground for the work that is only ours to do. And it is the most intimate level at which we can practice becoming a regenerative species.
