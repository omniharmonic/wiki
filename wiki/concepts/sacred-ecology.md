# Sacred Ecology

## Definition

Sacred Ecology is a plural, relational way of perceiving the beingness and sentience of the more-than-human world — recognizing that the tree, the watershed, the soil community, and the forest each possess a quality of beingness we can actually connect to. It requires no metaphysical leap or panpsychist commitment: the same phenomena that constitute our own life — metabolism, the dynamic sensing of and responding to environment — are present in other living forms, in their own very different modes. Sacred Ecology is the felt, embodied perception of the web of relationship as a living reality rather than an abstraction, available to anyone who slows down enough to perceive it.

## Entraining to the Cycles

Sacred Ecology is recovered through entrainment to the natural cycles — day and night, the lunar month, the solar seasons, and the longer rhythms of local ecology: when the cottonwoods flower, when the migratory birds return, when the rivers swell with snowmelt. These cycles are the base-code of existence, "literal metaphors" that point to transpersonal patterns while simultaneously being those patterns. Learning the cycles of one's place replaces the linear, deadline-driven temporality of the separated self with a cyclical, nested temporality — quietly undoing the conditioning that keeps the [[concepts/story-of-separation|story of separation]] in place.

## The Sit Spot and Empathic Extension

A central practice of Sacred Ecology is the sit spot: returning regularly to a place in nature and simply being there, without trying to meditate or achieve insight. Over time, one begins to perceive the continuous, responsive conversation the whole system holds with itself — how the wind moves through the canopy, how the birds respond, how the light changes. At some point the practitioner's empathic capacity extends beyond the boundary of the individualized self, and observation gives way to participation. This is the dawning of planetary consciousness — not as concept or belief but as a lived extension into the more-than-human world.

## Re-Indigenization Without Appropriation

Sacred Ecology is bound up with recovering "blood memory" of our own indigeneity to Earth. Every lineage, traced back far enough, held ceremonial traditions of ritual recognition of the cycles and responsibility to steward them. This re-indigenization must not mean appropriating from living indigenous traditions; it is the inner work of remembering that all of us descend from people who knew how to live in reciprocity with the living world — captured in symbols like the Medicine Wheel and the Great Hoop of Creation.
