# Bounded Embeddedness

Bounded embeddedness is the first value a bioregional currency programs into money: the currency can only circulate within a specific geographic region. In a world accustomed to frictionless transactions, this looks like a radical regression — and that is entirely the point.

The petrodollar gave the world a universal unit of exchange, and that universality is what makes it a solvent: a medium in which every place dissolves into every other place, every forest into its board-feet, every neighborhood into its market segments. A universal currency produces an extractive monoculture, because value denominated in it can leave a community as frictionlessly as it entered. A bioregionally bounded currency is the opposite of a solvent. It is a *membrane*: value inside it circulates rather than evaporates, and the higher the velocity of money within the membrane, the more wealth the community actually holds. It is also a *firewall*: cashing out to dollars is possible, but the penalties are baked into the mechanics, a downward pressure on exit that makes extraction dramatically more expensive.

A membrane is not a wall; its defining property is selective permeability, and a cell dies as surely from a sealed membrane as from a dissolved one. What stays inside is the high-volume everyday provisioning — food, shelter, energy, care; what crosses is genuine surplus, exchanged along [[bioregional-trade|bioregional trade]] routes. And because a single bounded currency can itself tip into the narrow optimization of a monoculture, a bioregion needs a [[pluralism|plurality]] of currencies — including asset-backed currencies tied to verified regenerative activity — because a place holds a plurality of values. A currency is only as real as what backs it, though: a [[local-currency|community currency]] is backed by the productive capacity of the community itself, which is why the currency alone cannot complete the design and the enterprise must be inverted too.
