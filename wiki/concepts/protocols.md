# Protocols

## Definition

A protocol is any pattern of interaction among agents that enables coordination without requiring a central authority to manage every exchange. Borrowing Nathan Schneider's elegant compression, a protocol is one of the smallest packets of information that can be transmitted while connecting to an entire universe of human experience and relationship -- a way of doing something, compact enough to share, specific enough to be useful, flexible enough to adapt. Protocols encode relational intelligence into shareable, forkable, adaptable patterns.

## Ancient, Modern, and Emerging

The word carries different connotations across domains -- diplomatic rules of state interaction, computer-science standards for machine communication, medical treatment sequences, indigenous ceremonial processes that establish and maintain right relationship -- yet the core meaning is constant: a pattern of interaction that generates coordination. A Lakota protocol Benjamin encountered is disarmingly simple: before you ask for assistance, give a gift of tobacco. A single relational instruction that shifts the frame of exchange from extraction to reciprocity, creating accountability without enforcement.

## The Digital Precedent

TCP/IP demonstrates what even a simple protocol can achieve at scale. Because it is opinionated about *how* information moves but agnostic about *what* moves, it supports an almost inconceivable diversity of expression -- the entire architecture of the modern internet rests on this single pattern. Blockchain protocols like [[wiki/concepts/ethereum]] extend the logic to value and coordination, enabling programmable agreements between parties who don't trust each other on credibly neutral infrastructure.

## Protocols as Governance Instruments

Following Schneider, every act of writing code is an act of regulation; every protocol is a kind of law. Protocols function as a worldview or ontology -- the kinds of interactions they support are the interactions that will occur. This makes protocol design a political act, and protocol builders face some of the most critical ethical considerations of any technology builder. Protocol development must be collectively steered so that the protocols we rely on reflect the interactions we actually want to experience.

## In Benjamin's Work

Benjamin frames protocols as the central response to accelerating crises in [[protocols-of-place]], arguing that expanding ordinary people's capacity to coordinate depends on documenting, sharing, and forking patterns of interaction. The argument connects to his broader thinking on credibly neutral coordination infrastructure in [[what-is-ethereum-for]] and [[ethereums-future]].
