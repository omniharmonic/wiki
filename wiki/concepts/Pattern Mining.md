# Pattern Mining

## Definition

**Pattern mining** is the AI-enabled extraction of key insights, strategic intelligence, and transferable practices from interviews, documents, conversations, and oral histories — particularly elder knowledge and practitioner wisdom. In Benjamin's usage, AI agents ingest transcripts and texts to identify recurring patterns, tag entities, and create cross-references that feed into shared knowledge repositories. It is the technology that makes hard-won relational intelligence *light* — extracting it from specific contexts so it can be generalized into protocols and templates that other communities can adapt.

## Pattern Mining in the Protocol Librarian Stack

Pattern mining is the third stage in Benjamin's "DIY Protocol Librarian Stack" (see [[wiki/concepts/Knowledge Commons]]): after **recording** elder interviews or practitioner conversations and **transcribing** them, AI agents extract key patterns, tag entities, and generate cross-references. Those structured patterns are then ingested into shared repositories and federated outward. The extracted patterns become the intellectual substrate for bioregional [[wiki/concepts/bioregional-knowledge-commons|knowledge commons]] — federated systems where communities share intelligence without imposing a universal schema.

Concretely, Benjamin imagines agents ingesting transcripts from the Front Range bioregional podcast and [[wiki/concepts/Cascadia Bioregional Movement|Cascadia]] knowledge commons, identifying patterns such as "backyard garden networks," then using agent-mediated translation (without a universal schema) to federate those patterns across regions.

## Preservation of Embodied Wisdom

Beyond coordination, pattern mining serves a preservation function: it rescues embodied wisdom from elders and practitioners "before their generation is no longer with us." It addresses the reality that centuries of place-based organizing wisdom, ecological stewardship practices, and community coordination methods remain "trapped in organizational silos, buried in conference proceedings, locked in the heads of practitioners who don't have time to write it down." Mining surfaces that knowledge and renders it queryable, contributing to [[wiki/concepts/Knowledge Organizational Infrastructure|knowledge organizational infrastructure]] and the broader work of [[wiki/concepts/collective-intelligence|collective intelligence]].

## Making Knowledge Light: The Cosmolocal Frame

Pattern mining connects directly to [[wiki/concepts/cosmolocalism|cosmolocalism]] — what is light and transmissible (knowledge, patterns, protocols) should flow globally, while what is heavy and place-rooted stays local. Mining is the practice that makes knowledge light: extracting it from its originating context and generalizing it into [[wiki/concepts/open-protocol-libraries|open protocol libraries]] that other bioregions can fork and adapt. It sits simultaneously at the intersection of AI capability (semantic search, entity extraction, vector embeddings), commons governance (who controls the knowledge infrastructure), and regenerative practice (honoring and preserving intergenerational wisdom).

## In Benjamin's Work

Pattern mining is a core component of Benjamin's vision for AI-native knowledge commons infrastructure, developed across [[wiki/concepts/bioregional-knowledge-commons|bioregional-knowledge-commons]] and [[wiki/concepts/Knowledge Commons|Knowledge Commons]]. It appears in [[protocols-of-place]] as the engine for federating place-based intelligence, and in [[the-infrastructure-of-belonging]] as the means of liberating practitioner wisdom from the silos where it currently sits unwritten.
