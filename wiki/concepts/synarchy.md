# Synarchy

Synarchy, in the sense used here, is an order of collective organization beyond both hierarchy and heterarchy. The mystic Richard Rudd offers the distinction: hierarchy is the old order's way, authority descending from above; heterarchy is the flat order of equals discovering one another; and synarchy is the order beyond both, a collective grown so coherent it can move as one without anyone standing above anyone, the way a hive's intelligence lives in no single bee. The word is rescued here from its older and more sinister political associations to name a genuinely emergent, distributed coordination.

Synarchy describes what coordination looks like once a community internalizes the reality of [[interbeing|inter-being]]. It is not consensus imposed from above nor mere aggregation of equal votes, but the [[collective-intelligence|collective intelligence]] of a body whose parts are so attuned that coherent action arises without central command. In governance terms it is the marriage of [[subsidiarity|subsidiarity]], decisions resting at the smallest competent scale, with the binding care of solidarity across scales, realized through [[polycentric-governance|polycentric]] institutions rather than a single sovereign center.

At civilizational scale, synarchy is Joanna Macy's Council of All Beings made operational: not a government laid over the living world but the living world, ourselves included, learning to govern itself through us. It is what a [[planetary-superorganism|keystone species]] looks like once it remembers it is an organ of a body larger than itself and begins, at last, to act like one, the political face of [[syntropy|syntropy]] and the [[the-ancient-future|ancient future]].
