# Open Protocol Libraries

## Definition

An open protocol library is a living, governable repository of documented, shareable, forkable patterns of interaction -- the social DNA communities need to organize effective responses to crises. Rather than a static archive, it is an integrated layer of community infrastructure where the people documenting and forking [[concepts/protocols]] are the same people enacting them. The library is the seed bank; the community is the soil.

## What Goes In the Library

Disaster-relief spreadsheets and mutual-aid hub playbooks, meeting-facilitation methods refined over decades of organizing (Prosocial, Art of Hosting, Sociocracy, Open Space Technology), resource-allocation mechanisms prototyped in Ethereum Localism communities, identity-verification practices that build trust without centralized gatekeepers, and conflict-resolution protocols that let diverse groups navigate disagreement without fracturing. All of these encode hard-won relational intelligence -- yet almost none are documented in a way that makes them discoverable, adaptable, and evolvable by other communities.

## Governable by Design

The architecture of the library must itself be protocolized: governed by open standards, forkable by design, and resistant to capture by any single organization. In Schneider's language it must be a *governable space* -- a commons with clear rules of participation that prevent enclosure while enabling contribution, supporting the local adaptation that makes protocols useful rather than imposing one-size-fits-all templates. Frank Sanborn's Social Fabric project, organizing neighborhood-scale knowledge by domain atop a relational trust graph, shows what this looks like when it stops being theoretical and becomes operational.

## The Pattern Language

A repository alone is inert. Scaling beyond isolated communities requires a protocol pattern language -- a shared vocabulary, taxonomy, and set of descriptive conventions that let developers in radically different contexts recognize common structures, discover each other's work, and compose protocols into interoperable systems. Like the pheromone trails that enable stigmergic coordination in an ant colony, the pattern language is the connective tissue that turns a thousand independent efforts into protocol ecologies.

## In Benjamin's Work

Benjamin frames open protocol libraries as an ethical mandate in [[protocols-of-place]] -- distributed coordination infrastructure to fill the chasm left by failing centralized institutions.
