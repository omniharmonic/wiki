# Debt-Based Money

Debt-based money is money that comes into existence as interest-bearing debt — created not by governments printing bills but by banks issuing loans. Nearly all money in circulation is created this way, and the consequence hides in plain sight: if every dollar is born owing more than itself, the system as a whole must continuously generate returns in excess of what was lent, and that surplus has to come from somewhere. There are only two ultimate sources of value in an economy, human labor and the living Earth, so the surplus is driven from them. Extraction is not an unfortunate side effect of the monetary system; it is the debt repayment schedule of a civilization-scale ponzi scheme.

The design carries a second-order cruelty that Richard Cantillon noticed nearly three centuries ago: new money benefits whoever stands closest to its creation. Banks, asset holders, and the financial industry receive fresh purchasing power first; everyone downstream receives it last, after prices have risen to meet it. Money creation is therefore not neutral plumbing but a standing subsidy to the actors best positioned to financialize the world. When private equity strips a functioning business and leaves a hollowed shell, it is consummating the monetary system, not betraying it.

Together with the profit-consolidating corporation, debt-based money is one of the two machines whose joint operation produces everything experienced as the pathology of late capitalism. Its inversion is the first design criterion of [[bioregional-economics|bioregional economics]]: rebind money with life through bounded, plural [[local-currency|community currencies]], including asset-backed currencies whose creation *records* regeneration rather than *requiring* extraction to service it. See also [[sacred-economics|Sacred Economics]] on interest as the engine that converts gift and commons into commodity.
