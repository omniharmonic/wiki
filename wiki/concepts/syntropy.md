# Syntropy

Syntropy names the universe's tendency toward greater order, complexity, and life, the complement and counter-current to entropy. Where entropy describes the drift of closed systems toward disorder, syntropy describes the observable fact that open, living systems build up structure, coherence, and aliveness over time, gathering energy and information into ever more intricate and integrated wholes. The term has roots in the work of Luigi Fantappiè and Buckminster Fuller and resonates with the "negentropy" of Schrödinger and the self-organizing order of [[dissipative-structures|dissipative structures]].

Its significance is that it reframes life and order as expressions of a cosmic tendency rather than improbable accidents staving off an inevitable heat death. Read through syntropy, evolution is not merely random variation filtered by selection but the universe's persistent reach toward more life, the same impulse visible in a star gathering planets, a cell nesting into a body, and a community cohering into culture. It gives a name to the directionality that [[living-systems|living systems]] thinking detects beneath apparent randomness.

In the political register, syntropy becomes a design principle. A civilization that optimizes for the health of the whole, pushes its power to the edges, binds it with care, and roots the gift rather than the transaction is "syntropy as politics," the universe's own tendency toward order and life expressed through the one [[planetary-superorganism|keystone species]] that had spent its modern centuries running the other way. So understood, [[regeneration|regeneration]] and [[synarchy|synarchy]] are not utopian additions to nature but alignments with its deepest grain.
