# Bioregional Economics

Bioregional economics is the study and practice of designing regenerative, closed-loop, cooperatively owned, place-based economies within the carrying capacity of their landscapes. It is a subset of [[regenerative-economics|regenerative economics]] that grounds that field's living-systems principles into a particular place: not "food" as a global commodity but *this* foodshed, *these* soils, *that* snowpack, and the people whose diets depend on them.

The field it inherits has done most of its work at the layer of finance — instruments, investment vehicles, plural accounting. What bioregional economics names is the missing middle beneath those instruments: the *material* economy of a place, its supply chains, its ownership structures, and the metabolic architecture through which food, housing, energy, and care actually move through a community, developed *as one system*.

Its specification rests on four pillars and two commitments:

1. **Healthy, local, multi-capital circulation** — the wealth of a bioregion is the volume of value circulating within it, not the stock it contains; every exchange that stays local compounds, every leak is circulation lost.
2. **Cooperative ownership** — the accountability architecture of the economy: when the people who own an enterprise are the people who live with its externalities, extraction loses its structural advantage.
3. **Mutuality** — a change of economic mind that makes the third party in every transaction (the commons, the aquifer, the soil) visible and vested.
4. **Holistic wellbeing** as the economy's purpose and its measure — a new accounting, not a reporting overlay.

These rest on [[subsidiarity|subsidiarity]] and [[consent-based-governance|consent]]: decisions at the most local scale capable of holding them, and the refusal of coercion as a coordination mechanism.

The [[bioregionalism|bioregion]] is the scale chosen because it is the one band at which an economy can be ecologically bounded and socially embedded in the same act: large enough that its loops can close, small enough that its flows stay embedded in knowable, accountable relationships. Its two central design inversions answer the two machines of extraction — [[debt-based-money|debt-based money]] is inverted by bounded, plural [[local-currency|community currencies]], and the profit-consolidating corporation is inverted by the [[federated-cooperative-supply-chain|federated cooperative supply chain]].
