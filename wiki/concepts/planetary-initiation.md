# Planetary Initiation

## Definition

Planetary Initiation is the reframing of the global ecological and civilizational crisis as a species-scale rite of passage rather than a mere policy problem. An initiation is what happens when the continuation of who you have been begins to threaten the continuation of the larger life you are embedded within — a threshold that cannot be negotiated, optimized, or reasoned around, only passed through by becoming someone, or some civilization, capable of a different relationship to the whole. At planetary scale, Life is asking all of us at once to let go of a way of seeing that is destroying us.

## Crisis as Doorway

The dominant story most of us inherited about the climate crisis is, in a quiet but decisive way, wrong: it is not fundamentally a climate crisis but an ecological one, and beneath that a crisis of relationship. The carbon statistics are a symptom; the disease is a severed relationship with reality itself. This is what makes the moment initiatory. Just as personal crises arrive to catalyze ontological shifts — when an old way of seeing generates more pain than it prevents — the [[wiki/concepts/meta-crisis|meta-crisis]] is the macro-scale version: the moment when continuing the old pattern becomes more dangerous than the terrifying unknown beyond it.

## Death and the Relational Self

At the heart of initiation is a confrontation with death. A fragmented self experiences the end of itself as the end of the world, and so it clings, prolongs, and invests endlessly in its own duration — the mortality terror that drives transhumanist fantasies of life extension and escape. Initiation walks a person to the threshold where the fragmented self can die so that the relational self can take its place, realizing it is the river and the forest, growing and blossoming and then dissolving back into the totality. This is, in one reading, what [[wiki/concepts/christ-consciousness|Christ consciousness]] realized: that we are the wholeness we are embedded within.

## A Neotenous Culture Without Rites of Passage

Colonial culture fetishizes youth and neglects initiation, offering no meaningful rites of passage into adulthood and no recognized threshold where the concerns of the self give way to the concerns of the whole. A culture without initiation produces perpetual adolescents wielding civilizational-scale power. Mystical and indigenous traditions offer initiatory experiences — plant medicines, meditation, grief — to help us "die before we die," but these can only illuminate the doorway; the passage itself is one's actual life, walked through in every interaction and choice.

## In Benjamin's Work

Benjamin frames our moment as a Planetary Initiation throughout [[awakening-the-earth-dharma-part-i]], connecting it to [[deaths-evolutionary-initiation-into-life|death as evolutionary initiation into life]] and to the [[wiki/concepts/the-great-turning|Great Turning]]. The door, he writes, is open; what remains is walking through.
