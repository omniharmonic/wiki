# Bioregional Carrying Capacity

Bioregional carrying capacity is the threshold below which a place's consumption and its regeneration exist in true homeostasis — Kate Raworth's ecological ceiling brought down to the scale at which it stops being a metaphor.

As a planetary picture, [[doughnut-economics|the doughnut]] is indispensable and, precisely because it is planetary, almost unachievable. The ceiling becomes real only when it becomes a *particular* ceiling: not overshoot-in-general but this aquifer's recharge rate, this watershed's carrying capacity, this valley's soil budget — quantities a community can actually read and actually live within. That means accepting there is no elsewhere to which overshoot can be exported. At [[bioregionalism|bioregional]] scale the feedback loop between economic activity and ecological consequence is tight enough to be *felt* and responded to rather than merely reported and ignored.

Carrying capacity sets the outer edge of the bioregion: a bioregion is exactly as large as the loops that must close within it. Its inner edge is set by [[material-solidarity|material solidarity]], the relationships that must hold it together. The founding act of a [[bioregional-economics|bioregional economy]] is the determination of needs, capacities, and carrying budget — a radical reorganization, because the extractive economy has trained us to treat every desire as satisfiable from elsewhere and every consequence as exportable to elsewhere. In a bioregion there is no elsewhere.
