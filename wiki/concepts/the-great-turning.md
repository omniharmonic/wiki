# The Great Turning

## Definition

The Great Turning is Joanna Macy's name for the epochal transition from the industrial growth society to a life-sustaining civilization — the shift away from an economy premised on extraction, endless consumption, and the [[wiki/concepts/story-of-separation|story of separation]] toward one organized around the continuity and flourishing of life. It names our present era as a great adventure and a great responsibility: the compression of collapse and the opening of what comes after, where the old world has not yet finished dying and the new one has not yet been born.

## Grief as Doorway

Central to Macy's contribution is the insight that ecological crisis is not merely something to be solved but something to be *grieved through* as a doorway to remembering. As one of the great guides home for those from colonizer lineages, she teaches that the willingness to feel the full weight of what is being destroyed — rather than armoring against it — is what opens the capacity to act from love rather than despair. This is the work that reconnects: coming back to life, back to ourselves as Life, and back to one another as [[wiki/concepts/beloved-community|beloved community]].

## The Shambhala Warrior Prophecy

Macy's retelling of the Tibetan Shambhala Warrior prophecy gives the Great Turning a living image. In a time when great barbarian powers of unprecedented destructive force have arisen, the kingdom of Shambhala emerges — not a place on any map but a reality that lives in the hearts of the [[wiki/concepts/shambhala-warriors|Shambhala warriors]]. They go into the corridors of power where the weapons are made and dismantle them, carrying two instruments: inexhaustible *compassion* born of grief, and *insight into the radical interconnectedness of all things* — the recognition that these systems of destruction are mind-made and can therefore be unmade.

## Three Dimensions of the Shift

In Macy's framing the Great Turning unfolds along interwoven strands: holding actions that slow the damage of the industrial growth society, the creation of new structures and parallel infrastructure built on life-sustaining foundations, and the shift in consciousness — the cognitive and spiritual reorientation toward [[wiki/concepts/interbeing|interbeing]] that makes the new structures possible and keeps the holding actions from reproducing the patterns they oppose.

## In Benjamin's Work

Benjamin invokes the Great Turning in [[welcome-to-omniharmonic]] as the larger movement each Omniharmonic conversation is enmeshed within, and weaves Macy's grief work and Shambhala teaching through [[awakening-the-earth-dharma-part-i]] as foundations of the [[wiki/concepts/earth-dharma|Earth Dharma]]. It situates the [[wiki/concepts/planetary-initiation|planetary initiation]] within a hopeful arc toward [[wiki/concepts/regeneration|regeneration]] rather than dystopian collapse.
