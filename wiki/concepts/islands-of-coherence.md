# Islands of Coherence

"Islands of coherence" names the pockets of a new, coherent pattern by which a system far from equilibrium reincorporates into its next form. The phrase is widely attributed to Ilya Prigogine, often rendered as: when a system is far from equilibrium, small islands of coherence have the capacity to shift the entire system to a higher order. Whether or not Prigogine framed it in exactly those words, the idea follows directly from his science of [[dissipative-structures|dissipative structures]]: at the threshold of reorganization, local coherence, not global mass, is what tips the whole.

The concept reframes how large-scale change actually happens. The instinct of the old grammar is to seek critical _mass_, to win majorities, to scale by accumulation. But systems near a phase transition reorganize around critical _connections_ instead, as Margaret Wheatley and Deborah Frieze put it: separate local efforts find one another, link into networks, thicken into communities of practice, and then "suddenly and surprisingly" a new system emerges at a larger scale with powers none of the participants possessed alone. The coherent few do not defeat the old order; they _out-cohere_ it. The same logic appears in Bruce Lipton's [[imaginal-cells|imaginal cells]] and in supercooled water crystallizing around a single speck of dust.

As practice, the idea redefines the most consequential action available at a civilizational threshold. It is not to win the argument or seize the machine, but to embody a coherent fragment of the new world before it is safe or reasonable to do so, to become an island of coherence and then find the others. Held this way, faith in a [[the-ancient-future|more beautiful world]] is not sentiment but evolutionary selection pressure, part of what keeps that [[basin-of-attraction|basin]] from closing. It is the operational heart of [[emergent-strategy|emergent strategy]] and the [[the-great-turning|Great Turning]].
