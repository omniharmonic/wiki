# The End of History

"The End of History" is Francis Fukuyama's 1989 thesis, developed from Hegel by way of Alexandre Kojève, that the long human argument about how to organize ourselves had resolved in neoliberal market democracy, the final form of human government, the end point not of time but of _ideological evolution_. Fukuyama did not claim that events would stop; he claimed that no further fundamental antithesis to liberal capitalism could arise. As the Cold War ended and the Berlin Wall fell, the claim seemed plausible, the victory lap of a post-war order that believed economic entanglement would render war itself obsolete.

The thesis is usually treated as a punchline, falsified when history "resumed" on September 11th, 2001. But there is a deeper reading in which Fukuyama registered something real and then misnamed it. What he detected was that the [[dialectics|dialectic]] of history had stopped turning over, not because the system had reached a perfect destination, but because it had [[saturation|saturated]]: the global techno-capital [[egregore|egregore]] had metabolized every position outside itself, foreclosing the genuine antithesis a dialectic requires as fuel. He looked at an engine that could no longer produce a real outside and concluded it had arrived. He was actually looking at an engine that had run out of fuel, a condition Mark Fisher would later name [[capitalist-realism|capitalist realism]].

Read this way, Fukuyama is right after all, but about a different "History." It is not history-as-events that ends, which pours on as turbulently as ever, but History with the capital letter: the long dialectic of negation, the premise that every order is built on the defeat of the last. That engine genuinely saturates and stops. What begins on the far side is not the end of change but the end of change-by-domination, the threshold to a [[the-ancient-future|new grammar]] reached by integration rather than conquest.
