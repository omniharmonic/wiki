# Bridge Schemas

## Definition

**Bridge schemas** are YAML-based translation layers that allow distinct bioregional knowledge commons to interoperate without imposing a universal schema. When federated knowledge is shared peer-to-peer, AI agents use bridge YAML files to translate between different community taxonomies—preserving each community's own way of organizing knowledge rather than forcing alignment to a single dominant ontology.

## How Bridge Schemas Work

In Benjamin's [[concepts/Knowledge Commons]] architecture, local communities maintain their own taxonomies for organizing knowledge. When an agent from one bioregion discovers relevant knowledge held by an agent from another bioregion, the two negotiate via bridge schemas: the pattern is translated and added to the receiving repository under its own adapted taxonomy.

The canonical example: a Spirit of the Front Range agent surfaces a "backyard gardens" pattern, while a Cascadia agent organizes the same practice under "urban agriculture." Rather than either side imposing its terminology, the agents use a bridge YAML to translate between the two—each repository keeps its native categories intact.

This is the mechanism that lets [[concepts/Knowledge Organizational Infrastructure]] behave "more like plumbing than like a database." Bridge schemas route references between distinct vocabularies instead of demanding schema alignment, so multiple ontologies can coexist without collapsing into one.

## Plural Coordination Without Standardization

Bridge schemas operationalize a deeper commitment: plural coordination without imposed standardization. Within Benjamin's [[concepts/Holonic Nesting]] thinking, the architecture supports fractal governance from personal to planetary scales—each holon retains local sovereignty while connecting horizontally through bridge-schema translation. This is essential to [[concepts/cosmolocalism]]: knowledge flows globally while practice stays rooted locally.

The purpose is also one of [[concepts/decolonization]] and epistemic justice. By rejecting universal taxonomies in favor of negotiated translation, bridge schemas let indigenous knowledge systems, place-based wisdom, and diverse cultural frameworks coexist as peers rather than being absorbed into dominant categorization systems. AI agents act as brokers of translation across worldviews while protecting each community's autonomy.

## In Benjamin's Work

Bridge schemas appear functionally within Benjamin's [[concepts/Knowledge Commons]] and [[concepts/bioregional-knowledge-commons]] documentation rather than in a standalone essay.
