# Care Work

## Definition

**Care work** is the relational, non-scalable labor of tending one another. It is irreducibly relational: it requires intimacy, trust, and reciprocity, and those things do not scale to infinity because they are made of attention, and attention is finite. Care lives in particular bonds between particular people — not in promises made to humanity in general, but in practices carried out between humans.

## Care Cannot Be Made of Paperwork

When we made care the responsibility of bureaucratic institutions, we handed it to bodies structurally incapable of caring, because they can see only the category and never the web of particular relationships in which a person is actually held. A bureaucracy treats homeless human beings as a "homelessness problem" rather than as particular people, because the institution has no hands with which to hold anyone — hands belong to individual people. The libertarian's error is imagining we can do without infrastructure for care; the liberal's error is imagining that infrastructure can be made of paperwork rather than relationship. Neither is willing to pay the real price, which is presence.

## Re-Embedding Care in Relationship

The answer is not the rugged-individual fantasy of *no* systems of care, nor the bureaucratic abstraction that purchases distance from the very people we claim to care about. It is to *reroute* care out of faceless institutions and back into direct relationships in place — into bioregional organizing, local [[mutual-aid]], solidarity that has a face and a name. We do not want less care; we want care re-embedded in relationship. This is not a leap into an untested future but a reinvigoration of something we already know how to do — the church community that quietly fed a laid-off member — now scaffolded with better tools.

## Civic Solutions and Reciprocity

Re-localized care looks like communities organizing their own infrastructure: insurance pools, rotating savings groups, rotational labor, mutual aid — [[civic-participation|civic]] solutions that meet real needs without routing through financial transactions. Rotational labor works because of the relationship and reciprocity woven through it; strip out the relationship and it collapses. The contrast is jury duty: the same act that feels like sacred obligation inside a real community feels like a parking ticket when demanded by an abstraction. Re-embedding care is how civic integrity itself is renewed — one relationship at a time, one shared stake at a time.

## In Benjamin's Work

Benjamin treats care work as the relational substance that [[functional-sovereignty]] and solidarity exist to protect, developed throughout [[sovereignty-is-solidarity]]. It grounds his argument that care requires healthy individuals with surplus capacity to steward the commons, and that the most universal solidarity we can actually reach is place-based and [[interbeing|relational]], not abstract and bureaucratic.
