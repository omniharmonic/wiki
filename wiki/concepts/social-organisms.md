# Social Organisms

## Definition

A social organism is a self-organizing group that functions like a superorganism -- people working together toward shared aims, cohering, functioning, and reproducing themselves much as a living body does. In the OpenCivics ontology, if DNA composes cells and cells compose organisms, then [[wiki/concepts/protocols]] compose social organisms. The organism is made of the irreducible complexity of real relationships; protocols are the compressed instructions that allow it to be created, maintained, and reproduced.

## Social DNA

Every functioning group runs protocols whether or not it names them: how a reading group opens and closes, how a work meeting sets its agenda, who brings what to a potluck, how a protest march handles communication and de-escalation. These protocols are the social DNA of every community and movement -- compressed instructions carrying the accumulated intelligence of every iteration that came before. Each one encodes a hard-won lesson about what patterns of interaction actually work.

## Forkable and Evolvable

Like biological DNA, social DNA can be shared, adapted, and evolved. A disaster-relief spreadsheet template that emerged in western North Carolina might have nearly the right fields for coastal Louisiana, with a forked column added for who speaks Creole or which families have boats. The underlying pattern persists while its expression becomes indigenous to the new context -- evolution through selective pressure rather than central design.

## Why It Matters as an Ontology

Treating groups as organisms reframes organizing as the cultivation of living systems rather than the construction of machines. Because social protocols are reproduced through actual human relationships, they carry cultural context that purely technical protocols never can -- accountability, trust, reciprocity, and the slow accumulation of shared meaning that turns independent actors into interdependent communities. OpenCivics' development of a social organism ontology aims to provide the infrastructure to unleash the evolutionary potential of open protocol development.
