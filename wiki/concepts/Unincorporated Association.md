# Unincorporated Association

## Definition

An **unincorporated association** is a lightweight legal structure for commons-based, member-led organizations that maintain collective legal standing without the overhead of traditional incorporation. In Benjamin Life's work, it names a class of "non-corporate" forms — recognized under Wyoming state law and extended into the digital realm through the [[wiki/concepts/DUNA]] (Decentralized Unincorporated Network Association) — that let communities receive grants, own property, and act as legal entities while practicing distributed, member-driven governance. It is a tool for repurposing extraction-era legal scaffolding toward regenerative coordination.

## Composting Capital's Legal Tools

Benjamin situates the unincorporated association within his broader practice of "composting capital" — taking the legal and financial instruments built for an extractive era and repurposing them for regenerative ends. Rather than inventing entirely new legal forms (a slow and costly path), he treats existing structures like the unincorporated association as compostable material: their minimal incorporation overhead and member-led design make them well suited to commons stewardship. See [[wiki/concepts/compost-capital]].

A recurring move in his thinking is **institutional self-negation** — encoding limits on the institution's own power directly into its founding documents (articles, bylaws). Backbone institutions serving a bioregional commons become "translation membranes" between the dying empire and emerging regenerative systems: they hold legal standing to receive grants and own property, while binding themselves through distributed governance — networks, term limits, and network veto power — so that authority stays with members rather than concentrating in a corporate center.

## Encoding Governance in Code

In [[a-regenerative-accelerationist-manifesto]], Benjamin extends the unincorporated form into [[wiki/concepts/DUNA]]s and DAOs, arguing that tokenized ownership "can separate investment capital from decision-making rights, enabling cooperative structures at scales previously impossible." The legal complexity that historically limited cooperative formation, he notes, "can be encoded in smart contracts." Where Wyoming's unincorporated association statute provides legal recognition, the DUNA marries that recognition to programmable, on-chain governance — giving member-led collectives agency at scales that once required elaborate corporate architecture.

## Functional Sovereignty and the Commons

The unincorporated association expresses Benjamin's theory of [[wiki/concepts/functional-sovereignty]]: real authority arises from consensual relationships and shared stakes, not state-granted privileges. He connects these structures to Elinor Ostrom's [[wiki/concepts/commons-governance]] principles — defined boundaries, collective-choice arrangements, monitoring, and graduated sanctions — and to [[wiki/concepts/polycentric-governance]], where many semi-autonomous centers coordinate without central command. The underlying vision is of communities generating their own legal authority through peer agreements that enforce boundaries through relational consequence rather than force.

## In Benjamin's Work

The concept appears most fully in [[bioregional-coordination-sacred-work]], where unincorporated, self-negating backbone institutions serve as translation membranes for bioregional commons, and is extended toward DAOs and on-chain cooperatives in [[a-regenerative-accelerationist-manifesto]]. Its grounding in consensual, relationship-based authority connects it to the argument of [[sovereignty-is-solidarity]] and to the digital instantiation described in [[wiki/concepts/DUNA]].
