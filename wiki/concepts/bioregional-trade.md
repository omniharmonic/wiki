# Bioregional Trade

Bioregional trade is exchange between places arranged around what each place, by its nature, is actually suited to give — the inverse of the logic governing global trade today. It answers the objection that a place drawing its needs from its own soils and waters must be a poorer, walled-off province: sufficiency does not mean isolation, because the bioregion is a [[bounded-embeddedness|membrane]], not a wall.

The classical name for why places trade is comparative advantage, and in theory it is sound. But in the extractive order "best" has quietly come to mean "cheapest," and cheapest, followed to its root, means whoever can most completely hide the cost: suppress the wage, externalize the pollution, draw down the aquifer and leave it off the books. Cheapness is often just a measure of how well a harm has been concealed. Bioregional trade asks the ecological question in place of the price question: what is in right relationship to produce here, within this [[bioregional-carrying-capacity|carrying capacity]]? California's almond groves — grown on mined groundwater, roughly a gallon per nut, largely shipped abroad — are *artificial* abundance, a plenty that exists only because its true cost is charged to an aquifer that will not be repaid. Multiply that correction across ten thousand commodities and you have the great sorting a bioregional trade order performs: not the end of trade, but the end of trade waged against the earth.

The pattern is among the oldest in human economic life. Turquoise from the northern Southwest traveled south into Mesoamerica while macaws, copper bells, and cacao traveled north to Chaco Canyon; *Spondylus* from Ecuadorian waters was carried deep into the Andes; and Andean communities practiced John Murra's *vertical archipelago*, braiding a dozen ecological tiers into one economy. What remains once artificial abundance is subtracted is a leaner and truer exchange: real surplus moving like circulation in a living body, arteries carrying what each organ makes to the organs that cannot make it.
