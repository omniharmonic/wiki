# Metabolic Rift

The metabolic rift is the rupture in the exchange of matter and energy between human society and the earth. The term originates late in Marx's _Capital_, where he reached for the biological word _Stoffwechsel_ (metabolism) to describe human labor as the metabolism between humanity and the earth, and observed that capitalist agriculture had opened an "irreparable rift" in it: industrial farming shipped the fertility of the soil one way, as food, into the growing cities, and never returned it, mining the land of its life faster than it could restore. The scholar John Bellamy Foster recovered and named the concept, making it central to ecological Marxism. Writing in the 1860s, Marx had already seen the system devouring its own substrate, a microbe consuming its pond.

The concept is doubly instructive. As diagnosis it is prophetic, naming ecological overshoot a century before it became unavoidable. But its proposed cure exposes the limits of even the most powerful critique. Marx's answer was that humanity must learn to "govern the human metabolism with nature in a rational way", to _manage_ the exchange more scientifically. That solution leaves intact the deeper assumptions beneath capitalism: that the world is dead matter, that the human stands apart from and above nature, that progress means tightening control over a planet that is fundamentally _not us_. Marxism was a furious antithesis to capitalism that nonetheless shared its [[story-of-separation|grammar of separation]].

Set beside an Indigenous first principle, the rift looks different. From inside a relational grammar there was never a separation to repair; the land is kin, the river a relative, the metabolism a single living body of which you are an organ. The metabolic rift and the spiritual sickness of [[wetiko|wetiko]] name the same wound, a humanity torn out of the living exchange that feeds it, but Marx reaches for a scalpel because his grammar contains only scalpels, while the elder sees no wound to cut because, in hers, the cut was never made. Healing the rift is therefore not a better technique of management but an ontological shift toward [[interbeing|inter-being]] and [[regenerative-economics|regenerative]] reciprocity.
