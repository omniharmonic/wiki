# Material Solidarity

Material solidarity is the concrete, reciprocal relationship of care and provision through which real people hold one another up: the upstream farmer and the downstream consumer vested in each other, the housing cooperative that carries a member through a hard year, the [[mutual-aid|mutual aid]] pool a neighbor can draw on. It is distinguished from the ideological solidarity of people who agree with each other — it is the *structural* solidarity of people whose incentives have been braided together by design.

It is how Raworth's social foundation — that no one falls beneath a life of dignity — is actually met at the scale where people live, rather than by abstract redistribution from somewhere far away. Solidarity of this kind cannot be scaled by decree because it is made of relationships, and relationships are not abstract: you can be woven only to people you can know, trade with, show up for, and be answerable to across a table. Push an economy past the scale of knowable relationship and its social foundation thins into ideology or bureaucracy.

This sets the inner edge of the [[bioregionalism|bioregion]]: it is the largest economy that mutualism can still bind. In a [[bioregional-economics|bioregional economy]], material solidarity emerges from the mechanics themselves — [[local-currency|community currencies]] that slow value, cooperative ownership that sinks it, and [[federated-cooperative-supply-chain|federated infrastructure]] that stores it, the permaculture principle of slow-sink-store applied to value. See also [[writing/sovereignty-is-solidarity|Sovereignty Is Solidarity]].
